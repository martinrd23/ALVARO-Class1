Present tenses

Simple Present

Use it for routines, repeated actions, facts and states.

Álvaro studies in the evening.
The exam starts at 9:00.
I understand the rule.

Present Continuous

Use it for actions happening now, temporary situations and changing situations.

Álvaro is preparing for his exam.
He is studying more this month.
English is becoming more important at work.
Exam clue: Look for meaning, not only time expressions. “This month” often suggests a temporary situation; “every Monday” suggests a routine.

Past tenses

Simple Past

Use it for completed past actions and events in sequence.

I took a practice test yesterday.
She opened the email and read the instructions.

Past Continuous

Use it for an action in progress at a past moment or background action.

I was studying at 8:00 last night.
I was reviewing grammar when my friend called.
Key contrast: Past Continuous often gives the background; Simple Past often gives the shorter event that interrupts or advances the story.
